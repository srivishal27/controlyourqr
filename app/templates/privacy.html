Privacy

The short version: we do not collect your data, because we never receive it.

Last updated: {{ current_year }}

The content you type into the generator — links, text, Wi-Fi passwords, contact details, coordinates — is encoded into a QR code by JavaScript running inside your browser. It is never transmitted to this server or to any other. There is no endpoint on this site that accepts it.

What we do not do

  • No accounts. There is no sign-up, log-in or password.
  • No cookies. The site sets none, of any kind, including "essential" ones.
  • No analytics. No Google Analytics, no tag manager, no pixel, no heatmap, no session recording.
  • No third-party requests. No CDN, no external font service, no embedded widgets. Every file comes from this domain.
  • No content logging. Your QR content never reaches the server, so there is nothing to log, retain or hand over.
  • No scan tracking. The codes are static. Scanning one contacts your destination directly and never touches us.
  • No selling or sharing. There is no data to sell.

What is stored on your device

Two small values are kept in your browser's localStorage, on your machine only. They are never sent anywhere and you can clear them at any time by clearing site data.

  • cyqr:theme — whether you chose the light or dark theme.
  • cyqr:options — your appearance defaults: error-correction level, export size, quiet zone and colours.

The content of your QR codes is deliberately not saved, not even locally. There is no history feature, because a shared or lost device should not be able to reveal what you encoded.

The network in front of us

This site is served from a machine we operate, with Cloudflare in front of it as a CDN and denial-of-service shield. That is a real intermediary and we would rather say so plainly than let you discover it in a response header.

  • Cloudflare terminates TLS at its edge, so it can see the metadata of your visit: your IP address, which page you asked for, and when.
  • It cannot see what you encode. Your QR content is never part of any request — it never leaves your browser — so there is nothing in the traffic for an intermediary to read.
  • Cloudflare's own analytics and script-injection features are switched off for this domain, and the site's Content-Security-Policy would block an injected third-party script even if one were turned on by accident.

If you would rather not involve a CDN at all, the project is MIT licensed and runs on any static host — or straight from a local copy, offline.

What the server sees

Serving a web page requires the web server to receive an HTTP request. Our web server therefore momentarily processes the same information every web server does: your IP address, the page path, your user agent and the timestamp. This is what makes it technically possible to send you the page at all.

  • These access logs are kept for a short operational window and used only for availability and abuse mitigation.
  • They record which page was requested — never what you typed into it, since that is never sent.
  • They are not joined to any profile, advertising identifier or third-party service.

Enforced, not just promised

Every response from this site carries a Content-Security-Policy header containing connect-src 'none'. That directive instructs your browser to block all outbound network calls from our pages — fetch, XHR, WebSocket, EventSource and beacons alike. Even a compromised or malicious script on this site could not exfiltrate what you typed, because the browser itself refuses to make the request.

Your rights

Data-protection rights such as access, rectification and erasure apply to personal data a service holds about you. We hold none, so there is nothing to request, correct or delete. If you believe otherwise, write to {{ contact_email }} and we will look into it.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the change will be visible in the public commit history of the source repository, alongside the code that implements it.
